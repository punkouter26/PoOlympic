Slope training review: Event 14, The Alpine Ramp

2026-10-14 night block · 4 athletes · a 15° ramp simulated as the flat track with gravity tilted by 15° · charts compare MATT's first 15° run (purple) with KIM's first 15° run (orange, resumed after a GPU error = dark grey) and KIM's slow-command 15° run (yellow)

Verdict: all four athletes now climb the ramp; the slow-command recipe was the fix

In one sentence: with their old walking brains nobody got up a 15° ramp (0 of 24); after the slope ladder every athlete finishes the 9 m climb in CPU heats, but they climb slowly (0.4-0.8 m/s).

What went wrong first: KIM learned to stand still on the ramp. She never fell, so the training looked healthy, but she ignored the speed command. Asking for slower climbing (at most 15 % of her flat top speed) fixed it.

1 · Following the speed command

Episode_Reward/track_lin (how well the athlete walks at the speed it is asked for; higher is better)

🧸 Toddler

The yellow line is highest. When we asked KIM to walk slowly up the hill, she did what we asked.

🎒 Child

This line shows how well the athlete walks at the speed we ask for. On the steep ramp, KIM (orange) only got about half of the points, even after a long practice. MATT (purple) did better. When we asked KIM for slower speeds she could actually manage (yellow), she got most of the points almost at once.

🎓 Adult

ks15_v1 (fast range 0-1.84 m/s): 0.30 → 0.51 over 764 iterations, 0.54 after the resume (the GPU fault at it 764 shows as the dark grey restart from it 700). ms15_v1: 0.45 → 0.67. ks15s_v1 (0.18-0.55 m/s, from ks15c it 799): 0.71 within 30 iterations, 0.76 at it 799. The reward is an exponential kernel of the velocity error, so a smaller command range raises it by itself; the event heats are the real check, and there KIM went from 0 of 4 (creeping at 0.1-0.2 m/s) to 6 of 6 in 26 s.

2 · Speed error

Metrics/athlete/error_vel_xy (difference between asked and actual speed; lower is better)

🧸 Toddler

Lower is better. The yellow line is lowest: KIM walks closest to the speed we want.

🎒 Child

This is how far off the athlete's speed is. MATT is the most off, because we asked him for fast speeds that nobody can run up such a steep hill. KIM's mistake got smaller with practice, and smallest when the speeds were gentle.

🎓 Adult

MATT ms15_v1 ends at 2.0, KIM ks15_v1 at 1.6, ks15s_v1 flat at 1.15. The metric sums the error over the episode and scales with the command range, so the runs are not directly comparable; within a run it shows whether the athlete keeps learning. MATT's stays high because his range (0-2 m/s) is beyond what he climbs (0.55-0.8 m/s in the heats): his brain is fastest when asked for 1.0-1.5 m/s (17.5 s) and stalls when asked for 3 m/s.

3 · Staying on their feet

Train/mean_episode_length (how long a practice attempt lasts before a fall; 1000 = never fell)

🧸 Toddler

All the lines are at the top. Nobody falls over on the hill.

🎒 Child

Every practice try lasts the full time, so falling was never the problem. The problem was that KIM stood still: standing is safe, and she still got points for staying up.

🎓 Adult

All runs reach 990-1000 steps within 50 iterations of the warm start from the 8° / 11.5° brains, so terminations carry no signal here. The failure mode was a local optimum: survival and posture rewards are earned standing still, and a speed command the body cannot reach pays nearly the same tracking reward standing as creeping. The useful levers were the command range and the intermediate 11.5° step, not the termination terms.
