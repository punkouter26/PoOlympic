Get-up training review: getup_rev_v2e

2026-10-01 21:09, iteration 1499 · MATT (mattbio body) · 1500 iterations in 72 min · getup_rev_v1 = the previous get-up run (orange, this afternoon) getup_rev_v2e = this run (dark)

Verdict: it gets up from flat on its back, every time

In one sentence: this is the first brain that stands up from lying on its back: 10 of 10 at every test from iteration 600 on, 30 of 30 on a larger test, and it never falls over afterwards.

Why it worked this time: the earlier runs sat down and stayed sitting. From a seated start the athlete kicked its knees open and sat bolt upright, because an upright body scored points and nothing scored for getting the feet under the body. This run pays for keeping the legs folded while the body is low, and pays for an upright body only once it is off the floor. With that, the seated start went from 0 % to 95 % success, and the remaining starts (further back, flat on the back, legs stretched out) fell within 50 iterations.

How it moves: knees up, sit up with one arm pushing off the floor, feet under the body, squat, stand. Joint speeds stay inside the 18 rad/s human limit (0.2 % of physics steps above it, 5 % allowed). The knees push at their full 280 Nm limit.

Two things to know: (1) the later brains are very fast. 0.7 s from lying to standing is quicker than a trained human (about 1 to 1.5 s); the iteration 600 brain takes 0.98 s and stands with its arms down, the iteration 1499 brain ends with one arm held out in front. (2) It was trained and tested alone on flat ground from lying on the back only, not face down.

Brain: provisional get-up brain = getup_rev_v2e_it600.onnx (the most human-paced one that passes). Not used in any event yet: Event 27 still needs its scene and the hand-over from getting up to sprinting.

1 · Standing tall

🧸 Toddler

The orange line stays low. The dark line climbs up high and stays there. The robot learned to stand up.

🎒 Child

This line shows how much of the time the robot is standing. Last time (orange) it stood up only from the easy start, so the line stayed at 2. This time (dark) the line sat at 2 for a while, then shot up between iterations 300 and 500. That is the moment the robot worked out how to stand up from sitting, and soon after from lying down.

🎓 Adult

Both runs sit at about 2.0 while only the easy 40 % of episodes (squat starts) end standing. getup_rev_v1's early spike to 4.2 is the squat-only phase before its curriculum moved on. In v2e the frontier (seated) episodes begin to succeed at iteration 300 and the line reaches 4.1 by 480, dips slightly as four harder stages arrive within 50 iterations, and settles at 4.45 of 5. The missing 0.55 is the 0.7 s it takes to get up out of each 6 s episode, so there is little left to gain.

2 · Practice stage

Curriculum/getup_rev/getup_stage. The two runs use different ladders: orange 0 squat · 1 kneel · 2 sit · 3 lying; dark 0 squat · 1 to 3 rocked further back onto the back with the knees tucked · 4 to 6 legs stretched out · 6 = flat on the back, the event start. The dark run starts at stage 2 because its starting brain already passed stages 0 and 1.

🧸 Toddler

The orange line stops on the first step. The dark line climbs all the steps to the top.

🎒 Child

Each step is a harder starting position, like school grades. Last time the robot got stuck in grade 1. This time it was stuck in grade 2 (starting seated) for a long while, then passed it, and then raced through grades 3, 4, 5 and 6 in a few minutes, because they all use the same trick it had just learned.

🎓 Adult

v1 stalled at its kneel stage with 0 % success for 1400 iterations. v2e's ladder interpolates the start pose between key poses (squat → on the back with knees tucked → supine), so each stage starts a small step from the last. Stage 2 took 489 iterations; stages 3 to 6 took 15 each, the minimum the curriculum allows, since each new stage is seen as a 10 % preview before it becomes the frontier. From iteration 535 the frontier is the event start and its success rate is 99 %.

3 · Lift assist

Metrics/getup_assist_max (upward helping force on the torso, as a fraction of body weight; 0 = no help)

🧸 Toddler

At first something helps lift the robot. Later the line goes down to zero: the robot gets up all by itself.

🎒 Child

The assist is like a parent holding a child under the arms. Last time (orange) the help faded on a timer whether the robot was ready or not. This time (dark) the help grows when the robot keeps failing and shrinks when it succeeds. It needed a lot of help for a long time, then learned the move and the help dropped to nothing within 60 iterations. All the tests are done with no help.

🎓 Adult

v1's assist decayed linearly to 0 by iteration 600 without a single kneel success before or after. v2e's assist is a controller on the frontier success rate (+0.03 body weights per iteration at 0 % success, −0.02 at 100 %, capped at 0.8) and applies only to frontier episodes. It saturated at 0.8 for about 390 iterations while the leg-folding behaviour was being learned (the assist alone did not produce successes in the three variants before this one), then fell to 0 between iterations 430 and 489 as success passed 60 %. The frontier advances only after five iterations at zero assist, so every stage was passed unassisted.
